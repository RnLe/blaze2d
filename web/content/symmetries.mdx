# Symmetries

A symmetry can save an entire solve, reduce the work within a solve, or reduce
stored data. These are different mechanisms. Blaze checks each against the
**material and the finite Maxwell problem actually being solved**. A lattice
label alone does not authorize a reduction.

The implementation covers exact affine operations for all 17 plane groups,
equivalent-wavevector reuse, ordinary and projective sectors, qualified PT real
coordinates, and selected reduced kernels. Each mechanism has an unrestricted
control and explicit eligibility checks. Some combinations remain unsupported;
some correct reductions cost more than they save. The
[replacement notes](/symmetry-changelog) record those limits and the measurements.

## The Maxwell pencil is the contract

After separating the Bloch phase, Blaze solves a finite generalized eigenproblem

$$
A_{\mathbf{k}}u=\lambda B u,
\qquad \lambda=(\omega/c)^2.
$$

For TM, $u$ represents the periodic part of $E_z$, $A_{\mathbf{k}}$ is the
Bloch Laplacian and $B$ is the sampled dielectric weight. For TE, $u$ represents
$H_z$, $B=I$, and the operator contains the sampled inverse dielectric tensor
between Bloch derivatives. Smoothing can produce such a tensor even when the
physical dielectric is scalar. Reported normalized frequencies also include
Blaze's length and $2\pi$ conventions.

Let $T_g$ map coefficients at $\mathbf{k}$ to coefficients at $\mathbf{k}'$.
A usable unitary symmetry must preserve both forms of the pencil:

$$
T_g^\dagger A_{\mathbf{k}'}T_g=A_{\mathbf{k}},
\qquad
T_g^\dagger B_{\mathbf{k}'}T_g=B_{\mathbf{k}}.
$$

For an antiunitary action, the corresponding covariance identities also
include complex conjugation.

Blaze checks the lattice metric, scalar material, inverse tensor, sample mapping,
retained Fourier support and operator action. A point permutation on a periodic
array is insufficient: wrapping a Fourier index can change its derivative,
especially at an even grid's Nyquist boundary. Qualification is specific to the
grid, material, wavevector and representation.
Affine closure and lattice carries are exact. Material/operator covariance and
field accuracy are numerical checks with explicit tolerances and rejection
reasons.

## Coordinates, translations and field signs

Write the direct lattice vectors as the columns of $L$ and use fractional
coordinates $\boldsymbol\xi$ and $\boldsymbol\kappa$:

$$
\mathbf{x}=L\boldsymbol\xi,
\qquad
\mathbf{k}=2\pi L^{-T}\boldsymbol\kappa.
$$

An affine operation is $g=(W,\boldsymbol\tau)$, acting as
$\boldsymbol\xi\mapsto W\boldsymbol\xi+\boldsymbol\tau$.
$W$ is an integer lattice automorphism; translations and origin shifts use exact
rational arithmetic. Its Cartesian action is $R=LWL^{-1}$. A point symmetry
satisfies $W^T L^T L W=L^T L$. This distinction between fractional and Cartesian
coordinates matters on oblique and hexagonal cells.<Citation id={1} />

Composition retains the integer lattice translation left by the chosen
representatives:

$$
\boldsymbol\tau_g+W_g\boldsymbol\tau_h
=\boldsymbol\tau_{gh}+\boldsymbol\ell(g,h).
$$

The carry $\boldsymbol\ell$ contributes a Bloch phase; discarding it changes the
representation. For a unitary operation, let $\mathbf{m}_g$ fold the transformed
wavevector into the chosen reciprocal cell. A Fourier coefficient transforms as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol\kappa'&=W^{-T}\boldsymbol\kappa-\mathbf{m}_g,\\
\mathbf{n}'&=W^{-T}\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{m}_g,\\
c'_{\mathbf{n}'}&=
e^{-2\pi i[W^{-T}(\mathbf{n}+\boldsymbol\kappa)]\cdot\boldsymbol\tau}
\,D(R)c_{\mathbf{n}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For in-plane operations, $D(R)=1$ for TM's polar $E_z$, while
$D(R)=\det R$ for TE's axial $H_z$. Thus a physical reflection label for TE
differs from the label obtained by treating $H_z$ as an ordinary scalar.
Antiunitary actions additionally conjugate the coefficients and reverse the
wavevector before folding. The physical time-reversal sign is also retained:
$E_z$ is even and $H_z$ is odd under time reversal.

The exact group catalogue includes centered settings in primitive coordinates.
It does not imply that every group acts on every rectangular FFT support. In
particular, a valid hexagonal rotation need not preserve a rectangular set of
Fourier modes. Unsupported actions are rejected rather than aliased into place.

| Conventional setting | Plane groups in the catalogue |
|---|---|
| General / oblique | `p1`, `p2` |
| Primitive rectangular | `pm`, `pg`, `pmm`, `pmg`, `pgg` |
| Centered rectangular | `cm`, `cmm` |
| Square | `p4`, `p4m`, `p4g` |
| Hexagonal | `p3`, `p3m1`, `p31m`, `p6`, `p6m` |

These are conventional settings, not an inference from the lattice alone. A
motif on a square or hexagonal lattice can retain only a smaller group.

## Reuse between wavevectors

The **star** of a wavevector contains points related by qualified crystal
operations. Blaze can solve one representative and reconstruct the requested
fields at the others. Identical requests have an even simpler reuse path.
For real reciprocal materials, qualified time reversal can provide additional
equivalences, but only orbits not already covered by unitary operations count as
extra savings.

Reuse preserves requested point order, weights, complex fields and numerical
certificates. An optional traversal changes execution order to improve
continuation, then restores the user's order. These gains have different causes:
star reuse eliminates solves; traversal attempts to make the remaining solves
easier. A sparse path with no equivalent points cannot obtain the former gain.

Cache entries carry the operator, support and representation identity. Only
matching finite operators qualify for exact reuse. Fields from nearby, different
problems may instead become
**seeds**, after rank extraction and Ritz preparation in the new problem's mass
metric. Several nonzero projected columns can still span only one direction.

## Sectors and complete band coverage

The **little group** preserves a fixed wavevector modulo reciprocal translations.
Its representations split the mode space into invariant sectors. Matrix units
can select individual rows of a multidimensional representation:

$$
P_{ab}^{(\alpha)}
=\frac{d_\alpha}{|G_{\mathbf{k}}|}
\sum_{g\in G_{\mathbf{k}}}
D_{ab}^{(\alpha)}(g)^*T_g.
$$

This is the algebraic basis for solving symmetry subproblems and reconstructing
their partners.<Citation id={2} /> At boundary wavevectors, fractional
translations can produce a projective multiplication law,

$$
T_gT_h=e^{-2\pi i\boldsymbol\kappa\cdot\boldsymbol\ell(g,h)}T_{gh}.
$$

Blaze matches the representation to that factor system. Glide sectors cannot
in general be assigned the ordinary reflection labels valid at $\Gamma$.
Complex characters remain complex where required.

Splitting the space does not determine how many of the lowest bands lie in each
sector. Blaze retains conservative reference prefixes and offers adaptive
occupancy with explicit guards and cutoff-cluster checks. Partner reconstruction
preserves physical multiplicity; equal frequencies are not deduplicated into
one mode. The scalar zero-frequency kernel at $\Gamma$ has one owner.

Returned modes receive fresh residual and mass-orthogonality checks in the
original finite pencil. Comparing degenerate modes uses their subspace, since
individual basis vectors may rotate within a cluster. A small residual alone
does not establish that the requested lowest bands are complete.
The eigensolver's stopping tolerance and these independent certificate
thresholds have different normalizations; a configured `1e-14` tolerance is not
a claim that every reported physical residual is below `1e-14`.

For fixed active sector widths $m_s$ and $N$ spatial coefficients, the main
algebraic opportunities are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Gram work:}\quad
N\left(\sum_s m_s\right)^2&\;\longrightarrow\;N\sum_s m_s^2,\\
\text{dense Ritz work:}\quad
\left(\sum_s m_s\right)^3&\;\longrightarrow\;\sum_s m_s^3.
\end{aligned}
$$

These are cost scalings, not measured speedup factors. Full-grid FFT work remains
roughly linear in the total number of columns. Sector guards, extra iterations,
projection and initialization can increase that total. Blaze therefore measures
the Gram/Ritz kernels, the shared iteration engine and the complete independent
sector control separately. A faster block operation can sit inside a slower
solver architecture.

## PT real arithmetic

When inversion and time reversal combine into a qualified antiunitary symmetry
$J$ with $J^2=I$, the fixed subspace $Ju=u$ admits real coordinates. For the
supported inversion gauge, these are real **Fourier** coordinates. An origin
shift changes the gauge; it does not authorize deleting imaginary parts from a
spatial field. A global field phase can be absorbed in the real gauge of this
isolated scalar problem without changing its spectrum. Translated antiunitaries
can instead square to $-I$, which does
not permit this packing.

Blaze keeps f64 scalar precision while replacing eligible complex vector,
Gram and Ritz operations with real ones. Separate TM and tensor-TE kernels act
directly on that representation. MPB also provides a real-Fourier inversion
symmetry build; that is useful context, not a prediction of Blaze's
speedup.<Citation id={3} />

Eligible persistent vectors need roughly half the scalar storage, with padding
and other allocations still present. Process RSS is measured separately. A
sector can compose with PT only if its projector preserves the qualified real
subspace. Complex rotational sectors retain complex coordinates where needed.
Blaze still exports the physical complex field.

## Storage reduction and reduced operator work

An orbit basis $S$ stores independent coefficients and reconstructs the full
field. The corresponding reduced pencil is

$$
A_r=S^\dagger A S,\qquad B_r=S^\dagger B S.
$$

If each application expands to the full grid, this saves persistent storage but
still performs full-grid FFT work. Blaze reports that mode separately from its
genuine reduced TM and TE mirror kernels. Those kernels reduce transform work
only for the supported mirror, grid and material combinations; reconstructed
actions are checked against the full operator.

The optional **reciprocal-metric support** selects a different Galerkin pencil.
A metric ball can be closed under rotations that fail on a rectangle. Its sector
control is the unrestricted problem on the same support. A passing projected
residual does not prove convergence to the rectangular problem or the continuum;
resolution and material-sampling convergence remain separate checks.

The public metric-support workflow currently requires f64 complex arithmetic,
geometry-sampled material and supplied path order. PT, star reuse and coarse
starts on that support are rejected combinations. Choosing raw material sampling
also changes the discretization: it cannot silently repair a smoother that
breaks the desired tensor covariance.

## Continuation and the later research tracks

The implementation also includes snapshot enrichment across geometries,
projected geometry-response solves, symmetry selection rules for those responses,
coarse-grid initialization, precision experiments, bounded sector workers and
parameter-chain scheduling. These have Rust research APIs and executable
experiments; they are not all high-level browser policies.

Their cost includes preparation and failed work. Response solves can create a
better initial field while increasing total operator work. Coarse starts solve
and lift a smaller problem, then extract rank in the fine mass metric. The public
coarse policy initializes only the first executed point and preserves the fine
operator and preconditioner. Precision experiments must pass fresh f64 physical
certificates. Parallel chains can lose continuation information and cache hits.

Each mechanism is compared against its own fixed control. Measured gains from
separate experiments are not multiplied. PT with reuse and PT with sectors have
explicit interaction experiments; several correct sector combinations regress.

## Configuring an experiment

The [Workbench](/workbench), native command and Python interface share the public
configuration contract. These blocks illustrate an optional PT experiment for
an otherwise complete, compatible calculation:

```toml
[eigensolver]
precision = "f64"
tolerance = 1e-14
max_iterations = 500

[bands.symmetry]
arithmetic = "pt_real"
k_reuse = "off"
k_traversal = "given"
```

Begin with a matching `spatial_complex` control. To test sectors, select a
qualified plane group and sector policy separately. To test a coarse start, add
an explicit coarse resolution and seed-field budget. Keep the fine grid,
material, bands, tolerances and output contract fixed. Configuration validation
and runtime qualification are both part of eligibility.

The [replacement notes and benchmark record](/symmetry-changelog) explain the
current positive results, regressions, timing exclusions and implementation
boundaries. No reported FFT element count or modeled payload is a measurement
of physical DRAM bandwidth or energy.
