# Geometry warm starts

Nearby photonic crystals often have nearby low-frequency eigenspaces. Blaze can
retain a certified space from one geometry and use it to initialize the next
calculation. The objective is lower **complete sweep time at equal accuracy**.
Fewer correction iterations do not guarantee a faster calculation: extra modes,
projections, material construction and certification all have a cost.

The native implementation offers copying, metric transport, secants, snapshot
enrichment, residual enrichment, bounded and reusable response predictors, and
periodic shape transport through the
same `bands.warm_start` configuration. These are explicit options. Current
evidence does not justify a universal automatic geometry policy. The existing
[symmetry policy](/symmetries) retains its own measured scope.

## The finite Maxwell problem

For each geometry parameter vector $p$ and Bloch wavevector $k$, the discretized
problem is

$$
A(p,k)X=B(p)X\Lambda,\qquad X^\dagger B(p)X=I.
$$

In TM, $B$ is the sampled dielectric and $A$ is the Bloch Laplacian, independent
of geometry at fixed grid, lattice and $k$. In TE, $B=I$ and $A$ contains the
sampled inverse dielectric tensor between Bloch derivatives. Material smoothing
therefore changes the discrete perturbation in different places for TE and TM.

The initial contract is a fixed grid, lattice, precision and physical spatial
representation with positive dielectric material. A changed grid, lattice or
representation cannot reuse raw fields as if they were the same coordinates.
PT, symmetry sectors, metric Fourier support and reordered k traversal use
their existing qualified workflows and report why native history was declined.
External sampled material is rejected by this configuration. Operator-extraction
tasks keep their existing reference and gauge conventions.

## One kernel at Gamma

At exact $\Gamma$, the scalar analytic zero mode is owned separately from the
positive modes. A donor stores positive modes and explicitly solved guard modes;
unconverged iteration slack is never a guard. The initializer projects target
constraints **before** extracting rank, replenishes missing independent
directions and performs Rayleigh–Ritz in the target metric.

In TM, even a vector orthogonal to the source kernel can have a kernel component
in the target dielectric metric. Source and target projections are not
interchangeable. The native snapshot retains enough information to do the target
operation correctly. A small nonzero wavevector is not treated as exact Gamma.

An exact repeated discrete pencil may accept an adequate certified initial
window with zero correction rounds. Changed material cannot obtain this shortcut
from a small residual alone: a collection of accurate eigenvectors can still
omit a lower band. The unrestricted solve, guards and boundary checks remain
necessary. They are practical coverage controls, not a global spectral-count
proof.

## The predictor ladder

| Method | Construction | Main extra cost |
|---|---|---|
| `cold` | Native workflow control without geometry donors | Guarded solves and certificates |
| `copy` | Previous certified same-k fields | Target rank and Ritz preparation |
| `metric` | Target-mass isometry | Metric application and normalization |
| `secant` | Aligned two-state extrapolation along a valid parameter direction | Small overlap factorization and block combinations |
| `history` | Bounded union of accepted snapshots or older innovations | Extra trial rank, actions and projected solve |
| `residual` | Target residual directions added to the copied space | Target actions, preconditioning and Ritz extraction |
| `response` | Approximate first-order source-pencil response | Bounded inner solves plus target correction |
| `tangent` | Reuse an accurately trained response from an immutable anchor | Training, bounded refresh and target correction |
| `reduced_response` | Solve response equations in a small complementary space | Basis construction, small solves and target correction |

For TM, diagonal mass transport uses $B_1^{-1/2}B_0^{1/2}$. TE's mass
transport is the identity. Neither removes the need for the target constraint
and Ritz steps. Secants align subspaces before extrapolating; a parameter turn,
poor overlap or missing history declines the extrapolation. Individual bases
inside a degenerate eigenspace are not physical invariants.

History can retain two full snapshots, or the newest full snapshot and a bounded
number of independent older directions. Set `innovation_columns` with
`method = "history"` and `history = 2` to select compression. The older
directions are an unlabeled trial space, not independently certified modes.
Discarding them changes the trial space but never the final accuracy contract.

## Discrete response and the Gamma complement

The predictor uses the actual coefficient increments between the source and
target pencils, $\Delta A=A_1-A_0$ and $\Delta B=B_1-B_0$. A source residual
identity makes their role explicit:

$$
A_1X-B_1X\Lambda
= (A_0X-B_0X\Lambda)+\Delta AX-\Delta BX\Lambda.
$$

In source mass coordinates, define
$\widehat A=B_0^{-1/2}A_0B_0^{-1/2}$ and
$\widehat X=B_0^{1/2}X$. The projected correction for column $i$ solves

$$
Q(\widehat A-\lambda_i I)Qy_i
=-QB_0^{-1/2}(\Delta A-\lambda_i\Delta B)x_i.
$$

$Q$ excludes the complete retained source window, including guards and the
analytic Gamma kernel. Mapping back also includes the first-order mass
normalization term $-\tfrac12 X(X^\dagger\Delta B X)$. Production inner solves
are bounded by `response_iterations` and `response_tolerance`; they do not
construct a full dense complement. Target constraint projection and Ritz
normalization finish the step, including the changed TM Gamma constraint.
Guards are copied while response work
targets the requested positive modes.

The second-order oracle results require sufficiently accurate source and inner
solves. Short production budgets do not guarantee that prediction order; the
ordinary target corrector and fresh certificate enforce the final accuracy.

Actual discrete increments matter. Blaze's legacy `analytic` partial-cell
sampling can have plateaus and jumps as an interface crosses sample locations.
Its name does not guarantee a differentiable coefficient map. The qualifying
Voronoi families exhibit the smooth regime needed for second-order prediction;
interface events and unsmoothed cells require separate checks. No continuum
shape-derivative order is inferred from a nonsmooth sampled map.

Same-k TM source actions can be retained with `retain_tm_actions = true`.
Validity includes the exact field basis and operator state. TE actions change
with material and are not reused by this switch. Final certificates always
apply the target operator freshly to the actual stored fields.

## Reusable responses (M7)

A `tangent` model trains an accurate directional correction at one accepted
anchor. It scales that correction by the displacement from the **same anchor**;
it does not repeatedly add the correction to successive solutions. A turn or
reversal, an exhausted reuse count, excessive displacement, drift relative to
the guard gap, or a poor target Ritz residual causes a bounded refresh. Failed
training remains charged and falls back to the ordinary donor. Unlike M6's
short approximate solves, a tangent is cached only when every requested inner
response converges at the declared training tolerance.

`reduced_response` builds a source-mass orthogonal complement $C$, excluding
all source modes, guards and the Gamma kernel. It solves

$$
(C^\dagger A_0 C)Y-(C^\dagger B_0 C)Y\Lambda
=-C^\dagger(\Delta AX-\Delta BX\Lambda),\qquad K=CY.
$$

The selectable basis sources are low Fourier waves (`fourier`), independent
older snapshot directions (`history`), and preconditioned discrete forcing
(`residual`). Low Fourier directions complete a rank-deficient preferred basis
within the explicit column cap. The small matrices, source actions and anchor
are immutable and share one admission lifetime. These cached TE actions belong
to the source response equation; they are never passed off as target actions.

Reports distinguish the small projected equation residual from the omitted
full equation residual. Solving a rank-eight model accurately does not imply
an accurate full response. Dense complete-complement and reused-tangent
controls recover second-order subspace error in smooth TE/TM cases, including
nonzero k and triangular cells. That mathematical order is separate from the
profitability of a bounded production space.

The model's anchor and products count against `max_cache_bytes`. If they do not
fit, the latest accepted donor has priority. Bank eviction does not invalidate
an already leased model. Model parameters live in `[bands.warm_start.model]`;
these methods require at least one solved guard. Guard separation is a practical
local check, not a proof that every lower mode was found.

## Periodic shape transport (M8)

The optional `[bands.warm_start.transport]` table registers the source geometry
with the target before the ordinary correction. For the inverse map
$y=\Phi^{-1}(x)=x+d(x)$, the periodic Bloch factor transforms as

$$
u_{\rm trial}(x)=e^{i k\cdot d(x)}u_0(x+d(x)).
$$

`jacobian = true` additionally multiplies by
$\sqrt{\det D\Phi^{-1}(x)}$, preserving the continuous $L^2$ norm of a scalar
pullback. It does not by itself preserve the target dielectric norm or the
sampled-grid norm. The target constraints and Ritz normalization remain needed.
This is an initializer for the unchanged target Maxwell pencil, not an exact
coordinate transformation of that pencil.

A rigid translation of the entire circular pattern uses Fourier phases and is
exact for the represented periodic field; integer-grid shifts use a permutation.
Subcell-shifted sampled material need not have identical spectra. For changing
radii and relative positions, compact periodic inverse maps send each target
circle interface onto its source interface. Their quintic transition annuli
have disjoint support. A bound on the displacement derivative below one
establishes global injectivity and a positive Jacobian. Large changes, overlapping
supports and insufficient transition space are declined explicitly.

Local maps use periodic cubic interpolation by default. `interpolation =
"fourier"` evaluates the represented trigonometric field directly at mapped
points. Its quadratic grid-point work is bounded by `max_interpolation_terms`;
it is intended as a controlled comparison, not an inexpensive default. Rigid
translation uses the exact phase path with either setting. Object order, lattice,
grid and physical Bloch fiber must match. A material-only change has an identity
shape map and keeps its ordinary initializer.

With copying, the transported source supplies the trial. With another predictor,
Blaze combines its prediction and the independently transported source in a
bounded target Ritz space. Transporting an already predicted response would
count first-order motion twice. All transported actions are recomputed on the
target, and the final certificate still uses fresh actions on the stored fields.
Map, interpolation, rank or budget failures retain the existing initializer.

## Scheduling and bounded ownership

`scope = "gamma"` is the default. `anchors` includes Gamma and the exact
reciprocal-fractional points listed in `anchors`. `path` uses a rolling same-k
bank along the full path. No extra k points are silently inserted.

Workers receive deterministic compatible geometry chains with cold roots.
Geometry-independent axes form separate groups. `parameter_order = "serpentine"`
reflects neighboring geometry rows while retaining canonical job indices,
requested parameter values and the supplied k-path tracking order. More workers
provide concurrency but also create more roots and shorter reusable chains.
Fixed chains can also leave workers unevenly loaded when geometry regions have
different solve costs. The final contrast studies show this can outweigh a
reduction in iterations; locality alone does not establish a good schedule.

`donor_selection` chooses `same_k`, `previous_k` or `screened`. Screening compares
cheap dimensionless step metadata before preparing one candidate. Its distance
estimate is a heuristic, not an error bound or learned profitability policy.
Previous-k history contains at most two native frames. Unneeded candidates are
not eagerly prepared.

The global `max_cache_bytes` allowance is divided among worker chains. Immutable
snapshots can be shared by the bank and path history; eviction cannot invalidate
an active donor. Reported bank payload, leased donors and path payload have
separate meanings and conservatively count some shared data more than once.
They exclude solver/library scratch, allocator overhead and collected public
outputs. `max_preparation_bytes` bounds predictor preparation per worker;
`max_predictor_columns` bounds trial rank. These are not a total RSS limit.

Guard expansion is opt-in through `max_guards`. It solves a lookahead mode and
grows the guarded window within that limit when the boundary is unresolved.
Every attempted solve is charged. Failed candidates preserve the last accepted
bank entry, then use bounded ordinary recovery. Cancellation releases contexts.
Checkpoint files contain results, not native spectral state: a resumed process
starts new cold chains for its remaining jobs.

A minimal opt-in composition is:

```toml
[bands.warm_start]
method = "reduced_response"
scope = "path"
guards = 2

[bands.warm_start.model]
basis = "residual"
columns = 8
max_reuses = 4

[bands.warm_start.transport]
interpolation = "cubic"
jacobian = false
```

Keep the same final accuracy, worker count and requested outputs in the reference.
This composition selects mechanisms for an experiment; it is not a profitability
recommendation.

## Accuracy and precision

Always request a final `bands.accuracy` contract for scientific comparisons.
Each mode must meet the relative **or** absolute residual limit; the entire
block must meet mass orthogonality and stopping requirements. Reports also
include dual-mass residuals, kernel leakage, parentage, cache decisions and
discarded correction work. Near zero, the absolute criterion is essential and
does not imply a small relative frequency error.

Explicit accuracy requests additionally qualify soft locks with fresh residuals
before deflation in both precisions. An inaccurate locked vector reopens instead
of disappearing from correction. Eigenvalue-change stopping alone did not ensure
accurate fields in larger f64 TE sweeps either. The original M0–M6 predictor workflows passed a
24² square/triangular TE/TM f32
audit with fresh f64 actions, relative/absolute limits $5\cdot10^{-4}$ and
$2\cdot10^{-3}$, and Gram limit $2\cdot10^{-5}$. The same request fails in parts
of the 64² covering set, including ordinary solves near Gamma. More iterations
do not reliably remove the native arithmetic floor. Use f64 for those cases;
f32 is not universally qualified at that contract.

## What the measurements establish

The repository archive `benchmarks/geometry-warm-start` separates correctness
repairs, infrastructure savings and predictor effects. Its `plan.md` maps each
requirement to evidence, and each result directory includes inputs, build hashes,
raw outputs and the comparison protocol.

- Constraint and rank repairs remove a reproduced Gamma warm-start failure.
  Persistent infrastructure reduces allocation count by about 96% in the
  measured 64² TM allocation control; complete runtime gains are much smaller.
- Small dense Maxwell oracles show approximately first-order copied-space error
  and second-order response error in qualified smooth regimes, at Gamma and
  nonzero k. Short production response budgets do not yield a useful total-time
  gain in the measured ladders.
- The final TE secant Gamma ladder reaches 1.18 times the throughput of native
  copying, about 15% less time. TM secants are flat in that final ladder.
- A 24² triangular TM f32 full-path case reaches 1.12 times ordinary throughput,
  about 11% less time. Fresh f64 actions on the actual stored fields confirm
  the declared accuracy. This is a narrow qualified case.
- Larger 64-geometry, eight-worker f64 sweeps are qualitatively flat or slower
  than the ordinary workflow. Existing automatic symmetry is about twice as
  fast as native continuation in the admitted symmetric cases. Native history
  also increases process memory there. Fewer iterations alone do not justify
  switching workflows.
- Compressed history lowers retained memory but did not pass the 10% speed
  gate. A full k bank was 21–65% slower than Gamma-only reuse in the initial
  four complete-path controls. Selected extra anchors helped triangular TM
  there, but the final held-out set does not establish a general anchor benefit.

The final archive contains 60 numerically passing comparison configurations,
separate field audits and explanatory profiles. The full-workflow promotion
gate fails because substantial regressions accompany the narrow wins.
Final direct certificates cost under 1% of summed solve-worker time in the
measured profiles; removing scientific checks would not resolve the scheduling
and preparation costs.

These are qualitative decision gates from paired whole-workflow runs on a
shared machine with lenient host-load admission. Rejected timing samples and
numerical failures are retained. Software byte counts are not measured DRAM
bandwidth. Linux is the main measurement platform; a macOS runner is unavailable.
See the evidence ledger for the exact build, accuracy, output and concurrency
of each experiment. `final/results.md` records the final ratios, admission
counts, costs and limitations; `validation/README.md` records Linux, Windows,
Python, WASM and browser qualification. No later predictor is automatically
enabled merely because its mathematical order is better.

The M0–M6 results above precede the M7/M8 extension. Both reusable response
models and periodic shape transport are now implemented as explicit options.
Their separate evidence archive is `benchmarks/geometry-warm-start/m7-m8`.
The earlier entry-gate rejection remains a historical measurement, not a claim
that these mechanisms are unavailable.

## M7/M8 decision gate

The extension has 74 numerically passing comparisons and 3,808 independently
audited stored field blocks. Of 182 measured pairs, 166 pass the lenient host-load
screen. f32, pure-contrast and follow-up training checks use one measured pair for qualification;
the main timing comparisons use three. These are qualitative results, not precise
hardware constants.

- Reusable tangents preserve the expected local prediction order, but training
  dominates the measured benefit. The 32² square Gamma sweeps are 1.53 times
  slower in TE and 3.26 times slower in TM than native copying. Correction
  rounds also increase slightly in these cases.
- Reduced spaces are cheaper, but no tested rank or basis source establishes
  a useful general speedup. A tiny projected-equation residual can coexist
  with a large omitted full-equation residual. The target corrector still
  enforces the same final accuracy.
- Exact rigid transport on the 32² triangular TE path reduces correction rounds
  by about 8% and total time by about 4%. This narrow result does not meet the
  promotion threshold. Local deformation is approximately neutral there.
  Close-neighbor square cases decline these conservative maps entirely. Wider
  spacing admits local transport on both lattices, with another roughly 4%
  triangular TE gain. Direct Fourier evaluation of local maps costs much more
  than cubic interpolation.
- In 64-geometry, eight-worker 48² square/triangular sweeps, combined reduced
  response and cubic transport save 19–26% of correction rounds, but take
  12–27% more total time and use about 52–55% more process RSS than the ordinary
  workflow. Existing automatic symmetry remains substantially faster.

The default 128-step tangent budget fails its training contract throughout the
64² Gamma cases. A 512-step control admits all 22 TM targets; TE training then
converges, but only 11 of 22 target predictions pass their residual gate. Total
time remains worse. The default reduced Fourier space also declines all f32 TE
radius trials, whereas the smaller contrast-step controls admit all 84 eligible
TE targets on each lattice. Correct final outputs through fallback are therefore
reported separately from successful model use.

Both extensions remain opt-in. Their mathematical qualification does not justify
automatic selection. The archive's `table.md` preserves every comparison,
`summary.json` contains work and memory records, and `results.md` explains the
profiles, constraints, failures and reproduction procedure. All final field
acceptance checks remain enabled.

## Relation to prior work

Invariant-subspace continuation is an established method family; see
[Dieci and Friedman, *Continuation of Invariant Subspaces* (2001)](https://dieci.math.gatech.edu/preps/DiFrContInvSub.pdf).
Snapshot-space prediction also has a direct precedent in
[Frame et al., *Eigenvector continuation with subspace learning* (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07090).

For Maxwell problems, prior work includes extrapolated spaces for Bloch
eigenmodes in [Börm, Köhl and Talebi (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00337),
and geometry-dependent mode tracking in
[Georg et al. (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.02978).
Their discretizations and problem settings differ from Blaze's scalar FFT pencil.

The work here concerns the constrained native implementation, exact discrete
coefficient increments, bounded ownership and complete-workflow evidence.
The measured second-order local response verifies the implementation; it does
not establish a new continuation theory or transfer another solver's speedups.
