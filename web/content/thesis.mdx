# Why Blaze2D Exists: Photonic Moiré Crystals

*The research Blaze was built to make possible: a two-scale theory of twisted photonic crystals.*

---

</div>

<div className="narrow">

Blaze2D is the computational engine of a master's thesis, *Photonic Band Theory of Moiré
Crystals: A Two-Scale Approach*. This page is a short tour of that research, what it built,
and how it motivates the solver interfaces.

## Photonic moiré crystals

Overlay two photonic crystals with a small relative twist and a new, much larger periodic
pattern emerges: a *moiré* superlattice, the optical cousin of the patterns behind twisted
bilayer graphene. At small angles, direct supercell calculations become costly:

- At **generic twist angles** the bilayer is *incommensurate*: there is no repeating unit
  cell at all, so a conventional solver has nothing to operate on.
- At the special **commensurate angles** where periodicity is restored, the unit cell grows
  as roughly the inverse of the twist angle, so a brute-force "supercell" calculation
  increases substantially.

<Figure src="/figures/thesis/moire-crystals.webp" alt="Photonic moiré lattices formed by twisting two crystals" caption="Twisting two photonic crystals produces an emergent moiré superlattice whose period far exceeds that of the layers it is built from." maxWidth="760px" />

## A two-scale envelope theory

The thesis sidesteps the supercell entirely. Locally, every patch of the twisted bilayer
looks like an ordinary crystal with a two-atom basis; only the *inter-layer registry*
drifts slowly from patch to patch. A single dimensionless **quality parameter** decides
when this picture holds: the twist angle $\theta$ measured against the stop-band width
$\gamma$,

$$
\beta := \frac{\theta}{\gamma}.
$$

When $\beta \ll 1$ the local crystal barely changes over the distance light needs to "feel"
the lattice, and a two-scale separation is justified. A systematic expansion in the scale
ratio $\eta = a/L$ (monolayer period over moiré period) then projects the full Maxwell
operator onto a few retained bands, producing an **effective Hamiltonian** defined on the
registry domain and *independent of the twist angle*:

$$
\mathcal{H}_{\text{eff}} = \Lambda + \eta\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(v^{(i)}\Pi_i + \Pi_i v^{(i)}\right) + U^{(1)}_{\text{rem}}\right] + \eta^{2}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Pi_i\,M^{-1}_{ij}\,\Pi_j + U^{(2)}_{\text{rem}}\right] + \mathcal{O}(\eta^{3}).
$$

Every ingredient is a physical field on the registry: the local band energies $\Lambda$,
the group velocities $v^{(i)}$, the inverse-mass tensor $M^{-1}$, the covariant momentum
$\Pi_i$ carrying the **Berry connection**, and the **Born–Huang** leakage potentials
$U_{\text{rem}}$. The twist angle re-enters only through the single scalar $\eta$.

The headline quantitative result is this **quadratic ($\theta^2$) bandwidth scaling** of the
lowest minibands, predicted by the envelope theory and independently confirmed by a
finite-difference (FDFD) reference that is itself checked against MPB.

## Projected operator data

The framework's real payoff is *geometric*. Because it works from full Bloch eigenvectors,
it produces spatially resolved fields (Berry connection and curvature, the Born–Huang
potential, velocity and effective-mass maps) that decompose the moiré physics in a way no
conventional supercell eigenvalue calculation can.

<Figure src="/figures/thesis/geometric-data.webp" alt="Berry curvature and Born–Huang geometric fields on the registry domain" caption="Geometric fields on the registry domain: Berry curvature and the Born–Huang metric. These are the quantities the envelope Hamiltonian is built from, and the reason the solver had to expose eigenvectors, not just eigenvalues." maxWidth="760px" />

## The local-calculation workflow

The envelope pipeline uses Bloch eigenvectors at many registry points and constructs projected velocity, mass, and derivative data. Blaze integrates these calculations with parameter studies and explicit conventions for coordinates, gauges, and retained bands.

MPB also exposes fields, eigenvectors, and group velocities through its [Python interface](https://mpb.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Python_User_Interface/). The research motivation for Blaze is an integrated extraction workflow and control over its numerical implementation.
