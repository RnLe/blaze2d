# Optimization & Roadmap

<p className="roadmap-introduction">This page maps the development of Blaze into stages, grouping related modules so that later work can build on a mature foundation. Each module is marked <StatusLabel status="Done" />, <StatusLabel status="In Development" />, or <StatusLabel status="Planned" />.</p>

Blaze was built specifically for a [master's thesis](/thesis), with a narrower
application than established reference solvers: two-dimensional photonic crystals,
repeated parameter studies, and projected Maxwell operators. It remains actively
developed, with broader capabilities and further optimization as the next
research priorities.

**MPB remains Blaze's strongest reference and principal validation target.**
Adopting and optimizing MPB's capabilities is a natural direction,
while retaining Blaze's emphasis on sweeps and operator data.<Citation id={1} />

Two research stages look further ahead. **Stage M** applies machine learning inside
the solver, and **Stage X** solves whole families of problems at once instead of one
eigenproblem at a time.

</div>

# Stage 1: Research foundation

Establish a reliable research core.
Symmetries and configuration warm starts are the next solver features.

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="core">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## 2D research core

<ModuleStatusTag module="core" />
</div>

Blaze's research core computes TE and TM bands for circular inclusions in positive
scalar dielectrics, runs parameter sweeps, and projects Maxwell operators. Native and browser
execution share a configuration format. MPB comparisons and convergence metadata
provide the baseline for further work.

</section>

## Symmetries

<ModuleStatusTag module="symmetries" />
</div>

Start with rotations and reflections that preserve the sampled crystal.
Equivalent wavevectors can then share a solve. Symmetry sectors and real-valued
representations offer further reductions where the material and discretization
allow them.

<Motivation module="symmetries" />

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="warm">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## Configuration warm starts

<ModuleStatusTag module="warm" />
</div>

Independent geometry jobs currently start afresh in Blaze. MPB's standard run also
resets fields, although explicit reuse is possible.<Citation id={2} /> Blaze already
reuses fields along k-paths and accepts native warm-start inputs. The planned step
is automatic reuse between configurations: correct the old subspace for the new
material and check its residual. Published methods for tracking Maxwell modes as
geometry changes provide a starting point.<Citation id={3} />

<ModuleBanner module="warm" />
<Motivation module="warm">Warm starts already matter within a single crystal's k-space workflow; published Bloch-Maxwell work demonstrates substantial iteration reductions.<Citation id={4} /> Extending reuse to nearby geometries offers significant speed-up potential, to be measured at equal accuracy across a full sweep.</Motivation>
</section>

# Stage 2: Generalize and optimize 2D

Fully generalize 2D geometry, improve solving, and support MPB workflows.
MPB remains the reference for the extended capabilities.

## General geometry

<ModuleStatusTag module="geometry" />
</div>

Add polygons, composite shapes, and imported dielectric grids, with clear overlap
rules and accurate material-interface sampling. Initially retain positive scalar
permittivity. Tensor materials, loss, and dispersion would need separate extensions
to the physical formulation.

## Solver optimization

<ModuleStatusTag module="solver" />
</div>

Tune the existing block eigensolver, preconditioning, memory layout, and precision

offers. Evaluate alternative algorithms against difficult or clustered bands,

<section className="narrow roadmap-stage-section" data-stage="3">

# Stage 3: GPU, 3D, and slabs

Build on the optimized 2D core to make large parameter studies more practical and
study structures beyond the 2D TE/TM model. GPU parallelism targets faster band
calculations, while 3D and slab models add polarization coupling and vertical
confinement.

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="gpu">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## GPU execution

<ModuleStatusTag module="gpu" />
</div>

Keep FFTs, material operations, preconditioning, and block algebra on the device
throughout a solve. Jin and Xie report **over 40× average acceleration** for their
3D finite-difference solver using CuPy on an RTX 4090 D against a NumPy CPU
implementation.<Citation id={6} /> Blaze's plane-wave solver needs its own measurements.

<ModuleBanner module="gpu" />
<Motivation module="gpu">Higher throughput could make large sweeps and training datasets more affordable. For example, Feng's neural waveguide design study trains on MPB band diagrams across varying geometries.<Citation id={7} /> Faster dataset generation is a useful application of a GPU backend.</Motivation>
</section>

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="maxwell3d">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## Full 3D Maxwell

<ModuleStatusTag module="maxwell3d" />
</div>

Extend to lossless periodic 3D crystals with vector fields, 3D FFTs, and material
sampling. The scalar TE/TM split no longer applies: enforce transverse fields and
handle the curl operator's null space, with a larger memory budget.

<ModuleBanner module="maxwell3d" />
<Motivation module="maxwell3d" />
</section>

## Photonic slabs

<ModuleStatusTag module="slabs" />
</div>

Guided-mode expansion uses the modes of an unpatterned waveguide as a reduced basis
for efficient slab band calculations, combining periodicity in the plane with vertical
confinement. **Legume implements this for photonic crystal slabs.**<Citation id={8} />
A similar extension could expose slab bands, fields, and radiative losses, within
the method's basis and perturbative radiation approximations.

<section className="narrow roadmap-stage-section" data-stage="M">

# Stage M: Machine learning

Learn the parts of solving that numerical design leaves open, not Maxwell's
equations themselves. Networks can solve differential equations directly, by
penalizing the equation's residual<Citation id={9} /> or by minimizing an
energy.<Citation id={10} /> For a single accurate solve, however, a systematic
comparison found that physics-informed networks did not beat finite elements in
solution time or accuracy.<Citation id={11} /> In Blaze, learned components propose;
fresh residual and orthogonality checks decide. Gains count only at equal accuracy
against the tuned solver, with data, training and inference included, since weak
baselines have made many reported speed-ups look larger than they are.<Citation id={12} />
Modules are ordered by expected leverage.

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="preconditioner">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## Learned preconditioner

<ModuleStatusTag module="preconditioner" />
</div>

In LOBPCG, the preconditioner turns each residual into a search direction, and a
small Rayleigh–Ritz step combines the best ones.<Citation id={13} /> Learning can
improve that action while keeping it linear and positive definite, so the
algorithm's structure is preserved. The first step corrects Blaze's existing Fourier
preconditioner with learned positive spectral weights, computed once per k-point
and applied through the same FFT kernels. UNO-CG modifies Fourier neural operators
so that such constraints hold by construction, which guarantees conjugate-gradient
convergence.<Citation id={14} /> Rescaling the preconditioner alone changes nothing,
because LOBPCG's search space stays the same: learning must change relative weights
or add directions. TE, with the material inside the operator, leaves more room than
TM, whose Fourier-diagonal operator can already be inverted exactly.

Next, a small correction space can capture the error components the preconditioner
leaves behind, as learned DeepONet bases<Citation id={15} /> and neural spectral
preconditioners do.<Citation id={16} /> The training objective matters: replacing only
the loss with a condition-number measure cut conjugate-gradient iterations from 116
to 33 in a 2026 study.<Citation id={17} /> Fully neural preconditioners come last.
Graph networks that predict factorizations,<Citation id={18} /><Citation id={19} />
graph neural preconditioners inside flexible Krylov methods,<Citation id={20} />
hybrids of neural operators and relaxation,<Citation id={21} /> and multilevel deep
Maxwell solvers<Citation id={22} /> show the range, but none is a drop-in component
for LOBPCG.

<Motivation module="preconditioner" />
</section>

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="tuning">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## Self-tuning solves

<ModuleStatusTag module="tuning" />
</div>

A band diagram is already a sequence of closely related eigenproblems. Blaze's
preconditioner can blend a geometric shift with a band-window shift taken from the
previous k-point's eigenvalues. An online learner can choose such settings from the
measured cost of recent k-points, with no training set and no large network.
Learning to Relax sets solver parameters across a sequence of linear systems this
way,<Citation id={23} /> and PCGBandit speeds up a single transient simulation with
online-learned preconditioner choices.<Citation id={24} /> Before building the learner,
run every admissible setting from identical starting states to measure how much the
choice can gain at all. The first cold start cannot benefit, and short diagrams may
not repay the exploration.

<Motivation module="tuning" />
</section>

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="initialization">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## Learned starting subspaces

<ModuleStatusTag module="initialization" />
</div>

A configuration far from any solved neighbor still starts cold. A network can
predict the low-band subspace of a new geometry or material, which LOBPCG then
corrects. NeuralSound pairs such a network with an LOBPCG refinement for vibration
modes,<Citation id={25} /> and locally subspace-informed neural operators learn whole
subspaces rather than individual basis functions.<Citation id={26} /> Training should
reward subspace agreement and small Maxwell residuals, not raw eigenvector entries,
whose phases are arbitrary and whose labels swap at degeneracies. For a few geometry
parameters, a small network predicting reduced-basis coefficients may suffice. For
general dielectric maps, Fourier neural operators fit; once trained, they evaluated
up to three orders of magnitude faster than classical solvers on their
benchmarks.<Citation id={27} />

<Motivation module="initialization" />
</section>

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="surrogates">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## Design surrogates

<ModuleStatusTag module="surrogates" />
</div>

Parameter studies and inverse design need many related Bloch problems, often
including projected operators rather than band frequencies alone. A surrogate
trained on Blaze's outputs can estimate these across geometry and registry space,
propose promising candidates and choose where to sample next; Blaze then solves and
verifies each candidate. Physics-informed neural operators add the governing
equations to training,<Citation id={28} /> and pretrained foundation models transfer
to new PDEs with little fine-tuning data.<Citation id={29} /> Inverse maps are rarely
unique, since different geometries can produce nearly the same spectrum. Surrogates
should therefore propose several candidates or be paired with a forward
model.<Citation id={30} /> Interpolation, Gaussian processes and classical reduced
bases are the first baselines. Stage 2's gradients complement this with exact local
sensitivities.

<Motivation module="surrogates" />
</section>
</section>

<section className="narrow roadmap-stage-section" data-stage="X">

# Stage X: Solution families

Warm-started LOBPCG converges in a few iterations at most k-points, so the remaining
cost lies less inside each solve than across them. A band diagram or a parameter
sweep is a family of closely related eigenproblems: solve a few anchors accurately,
compress their eigenvectors into a shared basis, and solve much smaller projected
problems everywhere else. Eigenvector continuation learns such a subspace from
snapshots,<Citation id={31} /> and a recent review places it among reduced-basis
methods.<Citation id={32} /> The concept is established; the research question is an
efficient, validated realization for Blaze's operators, symmetry layout and outputs.
Modules are ordered by expected leverage.

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="reduced">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## Reduced Bloch solver

<ModuleStatusTag module="reduced" />
</div>

For a fixed crystal, Blaze's Maxwell operator depends exactly quadratically on the
wavevector, because each shifted derivative $\nabla + i\mathbf{k}$ is linear in
$\mathbf{k}$. In 2D, six fixed operators therefore describe every k-point. **This is
an exact identity of the discretized operator, not a Taylor approximation.**
Projecting these operators once onto a basis compressed from a few anchor solves
leaves a small dense eigenproblem per k-point, with no FFTs for its eigenvalues.
Reduced Bloch mode expansion demonstrates the principle for periodic media, including
2D photonic TE and TM bands,<Citation id={33} /> after earlier reduced-basis band
calculations.<Citation id={34} /> The Shirley method does the same for plane-wave
electronic structure.<Citation id={35} /><Citation id={36} /> The basis holds the
periodic parts of the Bloch fields, and the reused object is a subspace rather than a
list of labeled modes: a smooth global labeling of Bloch eigenvectors can be
topologically obstructed.<Citation id={37} />

<Motivation module="reduced" />
</section>

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="acceptance">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## Residual-checked bases

<ModuleStatusTag module="acceptance" />
</div>

Exact operator structure does not make reduced eigenpairs exact: all error comes
from the basis. Because the operator splits into a few precomputed pieces, the full
residual of a reduced prediction can be evaluated from small coefficient vectors,
without rebuilding the field. Accepted k-points stay reduced. The others go to
LOBPCG, started from the reduced prediction, and their new directions enrich the
basis; overlapping local bases take over when one basis grows too large. Simultaneous
reduced bases for several eigenvalues, with residual estimators, greedy sampling and
changing multiplicities, provide the theory.<Citation id={38} /> A 2026 study of
photonic waveguide modes shows why the check matters: once boundaries moved,
accurate-looking eigenvalues and fields still had substantial residuals.<Citation id={39} />
A small residual proves closeness to some eigenvalue, not that it belongs to the
intended band, so cluster tracking, buffer modes and occasional full solves remain
necessary.

<Motivation module="acceptance" />
</section>

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="sweeps">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## Families across sweeps

<ModuleStatusTag module="sweeps" />
</div>

Parameter sweeps extend the idea beyond k. With fixed interfaces and changing
material values, a few coefficient masks may again separate the operator, provided
Blaze's interface-smoothed coefficients depend on them simply. For moving radii or
interfaces, a change that touches only a few grid cells updates the projected
matrices exactly from those cells, at a cost that grows with the changed cells
rather than the full grid. Reduced bases for Maxwell eigenproblems on
parameter-dependent domains<Citation id={40} /> and a September 2026 study of
hyper-reduced and exact local operator updates for waveguide mode
solvers<Citation id={41} /> explore these routes. Moving sharp features can require
many basis vectors even for a single parameter.<Citation id={42} /> Local bases,
interpolation between subspaces on the Grassmann manifold<Citation id={43} /> and
registration of moving features before compression<Citation id={44} /> address this.
Unlike configuration warm starts, which still run a full solve per configuration,
the goal is to replace most solves.

<Motivation module="sweeps" />
</section>

<section className="roadmap-module" data-module="simultaneous">
<div className="roadmap-module-heading">

## Simultaneous iteration

<ModuleStatusTag module="simultaneous" />
</div>

Solving everything at once does not mean one huge block eigenproblem, which would
add no useful coupling. It means a shared search space: instead of fully converging
each new anchor, compress the residual blocks of many parameters together, add their
dominant directions to the common basis, and repeat. The Tensor-Krylov method runs
residual Arnoldi iterations for a whole grid of parameters at once, exploiting
low-rank structure across them.<Citation id={45} /> Extending this from one extreme
eigenvalue to clustered, generalized Maxwell eigenblocks is open research.

<Motivation module="simultaneous" />
</section>
</section>
