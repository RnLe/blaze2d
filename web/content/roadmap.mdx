# Optimization & Roadmap

Blaze2D focuses on parameter studies of two-dimensional photonic crystals and
projected operator and derivative extraction. The shared configuration and result
contracts connect the native solver, Python, and the browser Workbench.

## Current scope

- Two-dimensional periodic lattices with circular inclusions and positive scalar permittivity.
- TE and TM band structures, explicit sampling paths, and ordered parameter sweeps.
- Projected velocity, mass, registry derivatives, and the existing TE/TM research quantities.
- Registry studies and k-stencils with recorded gauge conventions and residual certification.
- Native f32 and f64 calculations; f64 calculations in the browser.

## Numerical and performance work

Performance changes need comparisons at matched discretization, solver settings,
convergence requirements, precision, and retained outputs. Priorities include:

- Stronger residual-based stopping and certification studies, especially for remote bands.
- Preconditioner and block-size studies for difficult dielectric contrasts and clustered eigenvalues.
- Warm starts between related parameter configurations, with explicit gauge and failure handling.
- Memory layout, FFT scheduling, and symmetry reduction where measured workloads justify them.
- GPU execution where arithmetic intensity and data residency outweigh transfer and launch costs.

The current solver uses LOBPCG. Alternative eigensolvers and electromagnetic
formulations require separate numerical validation. Browser execution is already
available; it uses the same scientific contract as the native interfaces.

## Geometry, dimensions, and compatibility

Future work includes additional geometry and material variants, general three-dimensional
Maxwell calculations, and an MPB compatibility adapter. These are planned extensions,
not capabilities of the current release. Tagged types, dimension-aware coordinates,
and explicit capabilities keep their future interfaces separate from current field meanings.

MPB-compatible geometry also requires a dedicated study of overlap precedence,
material sampling, boundary interfaces, and numerical convergence. The current
circle rasterizer does not promise MPB overlap semantics.

MPB already exposes fields, eigenvectors, and group velocities. Blaze's research
focus is the integration of configuration sweeps with projected operator and
derivative datasets for multiband models. See the
[MPB Python interface](https://mpb.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Python_User_Interface/)
for the reference capabilities.
